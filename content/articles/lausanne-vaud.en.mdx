**Lausanne and the canton of Vaud bring together global sporting institutions, specialist businesses, universities and places to practise sport within one region.** Their proximity creates practical opportunities to develop projects, find partners and connect ideas across sport, business and society.<Ref n="6" />

I was born and grew up in Lausanne. After twenty years at UEFA and the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI), I have come to appreciate this region even more. I also see considerable potential in bringing more of its strengths together.

## Why Lausanne holds a special place in world sport

The International Olympic Committee has been based in Lausanne since **1915**. The city has officially held the title of **Olympic Capital since 1994**. That history gives it an enduring role in international sport.<Ref n="2" />

Around the IOC, expertise has developed in sports administration, events, media, law and professional services. The Court of Arbitration for Sport, established in Lausanne, adds recognised expertise in arbitration and mediation.<Ref n="27" />

Many federations also work side by side. The [Maison du Sport International](https://msi-lausanne.ch) (MSI), on the lakeshore at Avenue de Rhodanie, opened in June 2006 and is owned in equal parts by the City of Lausanne, the Canton of Vaud and the IOC. It houses the headquarters of some thirty international sports federations and organisations, alongside around twenty companies active in sport.<Ref n="32" /><Ref n="33" /> For an organisation considering the region, it is a natural first address to visit.

## A sporting region stretching from Nyon to Aigle

Lausanne gives the Olympic Capital its name. The canton of Vaud gives it considerable breadth. Several centres of decision-making are based in other municipalities, along Lake Geneva or towards the Alps.

Mies alone is home to two international federations: FIBA, and the FIM, the International Motorcycling Federation, based there since 1994, which opened new headquarters in the village in 2024.<Ref n="28" /><Ref n="31" />

A business on the Vaud Riviera, a municipality in the north of the canton or a service provider on La Côte may have a useful role in an international sports project. Depending on the need, that might involve hospitality, manufacturing, communications, transport or event delivery.

## Economic benefits reaching local businesses

AISTS, the International Academy of Sport Science and Technology, studied the impact of 53 international sports organisations present in Switzerland over **2014 to 2019**. The study was published in 2021.<Ref n="7" />

<Block id="impact" />

The study identifies effects on business tourism and construction. For a hotelier, a tradesperson or a service business, those needs give international sport a tangible local dimension.<Ref n="8" />

The local opportunity lies in making these needs more visible and helping relevant skills find their way to a project. A small business can provide a highly useful service when it understands the sports client's expectations, deadlines and requirements.

## Where research can meet the needs of sport

The word ‘ecosystem’ can sound abstract. Here, it describes something straightforward: people with different skills who can work on the same problem.

At EPFL, the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Lausanne, more than 40 laboratories have contributed to sports research. Their work covers equipment, motion measurement, data analysis and the spectator experience.<Ref n="9" />

<Figure id="epfl" />

At the University of Lausanne, the Institute of Sport Sciences combines social and life sciences. Its work ranges from physiology to governance and questions surrounding Paralympic sport.<Ref n="10" />

Lausanne University Hospital, CHUV, adds sports medicine for amateur and professional athletes. Its services for adults and young people hold the Swiss Olympic Medical Center label.<Ref n="11" />

<Block id="expertise" />

[AISTS](https://aists.org/) illustrates this ability to work together. Founded in 2000, its eight founding institutions include the IOC, EPFL, UNIL, the University of Geneva, IMD, EHL, the City of Lausanne and the Canton of Vaud. Connections between sport, science, management and hospitality were built into its foundations.<Ref n="12" />

The network continues to develop. On 21 April 2026, the Canton brought sports technology stakeholders together and presented the Olympic Capital Innovation Challenge, developed with [ThinkSport](https://thinksport.org/), Smartmove and the [Lausanne Olympic Capital Foundation](https://olympiccapital.ch/). Its aims include testing and validating solutions in practical settings.<Ref n="14" />

The potential is exciting. An innovation becomes useful when a team can try it, a real problem is solved and someone can then adopt it within a sensible budget.

## Sport is part of everyday life

A sporting region also draws strength from what happens after school, during lunch breaks and at weekends. Lake Geneva, the Jura and the Vaud Alps offer opportunities to be active throughout daily life: walking, running, cycling, swimming or skiing, depending on the location and season.

Nationally, **52% of residents aged 15 and over take part in sport several times a week, for at least three hours in total**, according to Sport Suisse 2026. This figure covers Switzerland as a whole.<Ref n="15" />

The Lausanne 2025 Swiss Federal Gymnastics Festival offers another glimpse of that culture. In its final report, the organiser recorded nearly **65,000 gymnasts** and **300,000 visitors**. Gymnastics clubs and volunteers played an essential role in delivering the event.<Ref n="16" />

<Figure id="marathon" />

For innovation, this sporting life creates valuable opportunities to:

- listen to the needs of clubs and their volunteers;
- design simpler services for families;
- test equipment in real conditions;
- make events more accessible;
- help more people find an activity they enjoy.

A region that welcomes both sporting decision-makers and participants can sustain a particularly valuable conversation between international ambition and everyday experience.

## Quality of life matters to teams and families

Deciding where to work or settle involves an entire life. Journeys, housing, schools, healthcare and children's activities all matter over time.

Lausanne and Vaud enjoy a remarkable natural setting, with Lake Geneva and the Lavaux vineyard terraces, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since **2007**. Geneva Airport is approximately **60 kilometres** from Lausanne, with rail access to the region.<Ref n="20" /><Ref n="21" />

<Figure id="lavaux" />

In 2026, Lausanne ranks **7th out of 148 cities in the IMD Smart City Index**. The index reflects residents' perceptions of infrastructure and technological services in areas such as mobility, health and governance. It provides one defined perspective on urban life.<Ref n="17" /><Ref n="18" />

The security and stability of the Swiss setting also contribute to the region's appeal. Switzerland ranks **3rd in the 2026 Global Peace Index**, published by the Institute for Economics and Peace. This is a national measure of peacefulness, distinct from an assessment of safety in every Lausanne neighbourhood.<Ref n="19" />

Relocation still calls for a realistic budget, particularly for housing and operating costs. A good understanding of the region helps an organisation choose a location suited to its team and resources.

## Connections worth building over the coming years

In my view, one of Lausanne and Vaud's greatest opportunities is to help needs, ideas and skills travel more readily between these different communities.

Several areas deserve particular attention:

- **Connecting federations with local businesses**, through clear needs and manageable projects.
- **Bringing research closer to users**, involving clubs, athletes and organisers early.
- **Understanding audiences better**, to offer content and services that encourage people to follow and take part in sport.
- **Developing sound partnerships**, with a clear purpose for the organisation, its partner and the people involved.
- **Making everyday work simpler**, using digital tools or artificial intelligence where their value can be demonstrated.

These opportunities reflect my reading of the region. They require listening, choices and follow-through. Physical proximity provides a wonderful starting point; collaborations take shape when everyone understands their contribution and expectations.

The purpose of that work remains deeply human. In paragraph 37 of the 2030 Agenda, the United Nations recognises sport's contribution to peace, respect, health, education and inclusion.<Ref n="23" />

That ambition comes to life through ordinary actions: welcoming a child into a club, helping someone regain confidence, making room for a person with a disability or thanking those who give their time. A region can help build a better world through sport at this level too.
